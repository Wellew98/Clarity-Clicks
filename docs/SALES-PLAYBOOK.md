## Module 1 — Features & Benefits

### 1.1 The distinction (and why reps get it wrong)

Three levels. Most people stop at the first two and wonder why the call goes flat.

| Level | Question it answers | Example from us |
|---|---|---|
| **Feature** | What is it? | "We optimise your Google Business Profile — categories, services, photos, posts." |
| **Advantage** | What does it do? | "It makes you eligible to show in the top-3 map pack for your suburbs." |
| **Benefit** | What does it mean *for you*? | "When someone in Edenvale searches 'gas near me' at 6pm, your phone rings instead of your competitor's." |

Rules for the call:
- A feature is about **us**. A benefit is about **them** — their money, their time, their risk, their stress.
- Benefits must be **theirs specifically**. "More leads" is generic. "Refill orders landing on WhatsApp while you're on a delivery" is theirs.
- Never lead with the feature. Lead with the problem, name the benefit, and only then explain the feature as the *mechanism* — the proof that the benefit isn't a promise.
- If you can't finish the sentence "…which means you…", you're still describing a feature.

### 1.4 The one benefit that sits above all of them

Everything above ladders up to a single line, and it's already the site's positioning:

> **Not traffic. The phone ringing.**

Use this to kill any objection about SEO being vague. When a prospect says "I've been burned by SEO before" — they were sold *traffic*. We sell *booked jobs*. Say it plainly:

> "The last person probably showed you a graph going up. Did the phone ring more? That's the only number we report on."

### 1.5 Proof to attach to the benefits

A benefit without proof is a claim. Current proof inventory — use it, and don't inflate it:

- **Red Flame Gas (Cape Town):** ~5 new customers per day from local search; "gas near me" turns into same-day WhatsApp orders.
  - Testimonial (Emmanuel): *"People search 'gas near me' and now we're right there on the map. Same-day orders land straight on WhatsApp, and the site does the selling for us."*
- **MG Gas Company (Germiston):** found across Germiston and the East Rand; more calls and WhatsApp orders every week.
  - Testimonial (Liberty): *"We show up across Germiston now, refills and installs both. More calls and more WhatsApp orders every week. Properly worth it."*
- **Timeline claim:** many local businesses see more Maps visibility and calls within 30–90 days. Always qualify: a competitive Joburg suburb takes longer than a quiet one.

**Honest gaps in the proof — know these before a sharp prospect finds them:**
- Two named clients, both in gas. Outside gas, we're arguing from mechanism, not from case study. Don't pretend otherwise — "you'd be our first plumber, and here's exactly why the same mechanics apply" is stronger than a vague implication of depth.
- Both named results are Cape Town and Germiston, while the brand positions on Johannesburg. Fine — Germiston is East Rand — but don't overstate a Joburg track record.
- "5/day" is one client's number, not an average. Never say "our clients get 5 new customers a day."

### 1.7 Turning this into openers

Structure: **their world → the shift → the gap → the free audit.** Feature stays out of the first three.

**Cold call opener**
> "Hi [Name], quick one — when someone in [suburb] searches '[service] near me', the top three on the map get most of the calls. I checked and you're not in them for [suburb]. I do a free visibility audit that shows exactly where you sit and what's costing you the calls. Worth two minutes?"

**WhatsApp / DM outreach**
> "Hi [Name] — I ran a quick check on [Business]. You're showing up for [suburb A] but not [suburb B, C], and you're behind [competitor] on reviews. That's where the 'near me' calls are going. I do a free audit that lays it out — no charge, no obligation. Want me to send it through?"

**The AI angle (differentiator — few competitors are saying this)**
> "Have you ever asked ChatGPT to recommend a [service] in [suburb]? Try it. If it doesn't name you, that's a queue of customers you're not even in. Most businesses haven't looked yet — which is exactly why it's cheap to win right now."

### 1.8 Practice drill

For each of the six service features: say the feature, then "…which means you…", and finish it with something the owner would repeat to their spouse at dinner. If the sentence ends in a metric, redo it. It has to end in money, time, or sleep.
